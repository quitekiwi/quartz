## What Heart Rate Variability Is

Your heart does not beat like
a metronome. The time between
beats varies constantly — this
variation is called Heart Rate
Variability, or HRV.

That variation is not a flaw.
It is a feature.

HRV is a direct window into
your autonomic nervous system —
the system that governs whether
you are in sympathetic dominance
(fight or flight, threat response,
chronic stress state) or
parasympathetic dominance
(rest, repair, recovery, clarity).

High HRV means your nervous system
is flexible — able to move
fluidly between activation and
recovery. Low HRV means it is
stuck, typically in chronic
sympathetic dominance —
the state the managed world
is very good at producing
and maintaining. #verified

A joint task force of the European
Society of Cardiology and the North
American Society of Pacing and
Electrophysiology established
the standards for HRV measurement
in 1996. Since then HRV has
become one of the most widely
studied biomarkers in medicine. #verified

What the research shows consistently:

Low HRV is associated with
increased risk of cardiovascular
disease, cognitive decline,
depression, anxiety, PTSD,
and premature mortality.

High HRV is associated with
better emotional regulation,
improved decision-making,
stronger immune function,
and greater resilience
to psychological stress. #verified

## What Coherence Is

HRV has two dimensions:
how much your heart rate varies,
and the pattern of that variation.

Heart coherence is a specific
pattern — a smooth, regular,
sine-wave-like rhythm in the
HRV waveform, typically
oscillating at around 0.1 Hz
(one cycle approximately
every 10 seconds).

This coherent pattern emerges
naturally when a person experiences
sustained positive emotions —
appreciation, care, compassion.
It can also be deliberately
induced through specific
breathing and attention practices. #verified

When coherence is established,
something measurable happens
throughout the entire body:

The autonomic nervous system
shifts from sympathetic dominance
to parasympathetic balance.
Cortisol — the primary stress
hormone — decreases.
The immune system function
improves. Cognitive clarity
increases. Emotional reactivity
reduces. The body enters
what HeartMath Institute researchers
call psychophysiological coherence —
increased order and harmony
in both psychological and
physiological processes simultaneously. #verified

This is not anecdote.
It is measurable in real time
using HRV monitoring equipment
available in clinical and
consumer devices.

## The Heart-Brain Communication

Most people understand the brain
as sending signals to the heart.
What the research shows is
that the communication runs
primarily in the opposite direction.

Over 80% of the vagal nerve
fibres running between heart
and brain are afferent —
they carry signals from the
heart to the brain, not
the other way around. #verified

The heart’s signals reach
the brainstem, hypothalamus,
thalamus, amygdala, and
cerebral cortex — regions
governing emotional processing,
threat assessment, memory,
attention, and decision-making. #verified

What this means practically:
the pattern of your heartbeat
directly shapes the quality
of your brain function from
moment to moment.

A chaotic, incoherent heart
rhythm — the pattern produced
by chronic stress, anxiety,
and negative emotional states —
sends chaotic signals to
the brain that impair perception,
narrow attention, and reduce
higher cognitive function.

A coherent heart rhythm sends
ordered signals that facilitate
emotional stability, clear
perception, and access to
higher cognitive processes. #verified

This is documented in peer-reviewed
electrophysiology research.
It is also why ancient traditions
across cultures placed the heart —
not the brain — at the centre
of wisdom, courage, and
moral discernment.

## The Practice

Heart coherence is not a
complicated practice. It does
not require equipment, though
biofeedback devices can make
it more precise.

The core technique, validated
across hundreds of studies:

**1. Shift attention to the heart.**
Not metaphorically — physically
move your awareness to the
area of your chest.

**2. Breathe through the heart.**
Slow, rhythmic breathing —
approximately 5 seconds in,
5 seconds out. This naturally
drives HRV toward the 0.1 Hz
coherence frequency.

**3. Activate a genuine positive feeling.**
Appreciation, care, or compassion
for anything real to you.
Not forced positivity —
genuine feeling.
The emotional state is what
sustains the coherent pattern
beyond the breathing alone. #verified

Five minutes of this practice
produces measurable shifts
in HRV, cortisol, and
autonomic nervous system state.
The effect compounds with
regular practice. #verified

No cost. No equipment required.
Available at any moment.
