## What Was Buried and Why

The Library of Alexandria is the
most documented single act of
knowledge destruction in history.
Established in the 3rd century BCE,
it held an estimated 400,000 to
700,000 scrolls — the accumulated
knowledge of the ancient world.
It was burned. Multiple times.
By Julius Caesar (48 BCE),
by the decree of Theophilus
(391 CE), and by Arab conquest
(642 CE) in the traditional account —
though modern historians debate
the specific events and their
scale. What is not debated:
the knowledge is gone. #verified

The burning of the Library is
the most dramatic example of
a pattern documented across
the ancient world:

When one system of power
replaced another — politically,
religiously, or militarily —
the knowledge system of the
previous order was systematically
destroyed or absorbed and
re-attributed.

The Hermetic tradition survived
because Arabic scholars preserved
it when Europe had discarded it.
It returned to Europe in 1463
when Cosimo de Medici obtained
a Greek manuscript of the
Corpus Hermeticum and had
Marsilio Ficino translate it —
interrupting his translation of
Plato to do so, because Cosimo
believed it was more urgent.
#verified

The prisca theologia —
the "ancient theology" — was
the Renaissance concept that
a single divine truth had been
given to the earliest humans
and that traces of it persisted
across all ancient traditions.
This concept drove the Renaissance.
It was suppressed by the Counter-Reformation.
The knowledge did not disappear.
It went underground —
into Rosicrucianism, Freemasonry,
and the Western esoteric tradition.
#verified — that these movements
existed and preserved this material.
#credible — that what they preserved
was genuine ancient knowledge
rather than later invention.
