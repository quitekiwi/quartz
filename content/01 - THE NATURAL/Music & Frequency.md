## The Tuning Question

The international standard for 
musical pitch — A=440 Hz — 
was established by the International 
Organisation for Standardisation 
in 1953. Before this there was no 
universal standard. Tuning varied 
widely by region, tradition, 
and instrument.

Many traditions clustered 
around A=432 Hz.

The mathematical difference 
between these two standards:

432 Hz:
- Relates to the Schumann Resonance 
  through harmonic ratios
- C at 432 Hz tuning = 256 Hz 
  (2 to the power of 8 — 
  a pure mathematical octave 
  of 1 Hz, the base unit)
- Generates cymatic patterns 
  that align with natural 
  geometric forms
- Found in Tibetan singing bowls 
  measured archaeologically
- Verdi's preferred tuning — 
  he campaigned formally for 
  432 Hz as the Italian standard 
  in the 1880s and the Italian 
  government briefly adopted it
  #verified

440 Hz:
- Does not share harmonic 
  relationship with the 
  Schumann Resonance
- Adopted as international 
  standard in 1953
- Prior to 1953 the BBC and 
  US military had already 
  standardised to 440 Hz 
  during the 1930s and 1940s
  #verified

The honest position on 432 vs 440:
The mathematical relationships 
favouring 432 Hz are real. 
Peer reviewed studies specifically 
comparing physiological responses 
to identical music at 432 Hz vs 
440 Hz are limited. 
The existing small studies suggest 
lower heart rate and reduced anxiety 
at 432 Hz. Larger replication 
is needed. #credible → #investigate

## What Music Does To The Brain 
## and Body — Documented

**Dopamine:**
Salimpoor et al. (2011) — Nature 
Neuroscience. Using PET scanning 
and fMRI, demonstrated that music 
that produces chills causes dopamine 
release in the nucleus accumbens — 
the same reward pathway activated 
by food and sex. Music is not 
a luxury. It is a biological 
reward system trigger. #verified

**Pain:**
A meta-analysis of 73 randomised 
controlled trials (Hole et al., 2015 — 
The Lancet) found that music listening 
before, during, and after surgery 
reduced pain, anxiety, and the need 
for pain medication. Across 73 trials. 
Published in The Lancet. #verified

**Cortisol:**
Music in a major key at slow tempo 
measurably reduces cortisol — 
the stress hormone. Music in a 
minor key at fast tempo elevates it. 
The body responds to musical 
structure chemically, not just 
emotionally. #verified

**The Autonomic Nervous System:**
Slow music (below 60 BPM) shifts 
the ANS toward parasympathetic 
dominance. Fast music (above 120 BPM) 
shifts it toward sympathetic dominance. 
Heart rate, respiratory rate, and 
skin conductance all follow 
musical tempo. The body synchronises 
with the rhythm it is exposed to. 
This is called entrainment. #verified

**Neuroplasticity:**
Playing music — not just listening — 
strengthens the corpus callosum 
(connection between brain hemispheres), 
develops fine motor control, 
and increases grey matter density 
in multiple brain regions. 
Documented across multiple 
neuroimaging studies. #verified

DNA repair in humans.
