## What It Is

The term was coined by Harvard
professor Shoshana Zuboff in her
2014 essay and fully documented
in her 2019 book The Age of
Surveillance Capitalism: The Fight
for a Human Future at the New
Frontier of Power.

It is not a conspiracy theory.
It is an academically documented
economic model that has been
elaborated across hundreds of
peer reviewed studies since
Zuboff first named it. #verified

**The model in one sentence:**
Human experience is claimed as
free raw material for extraction
processed into behavioural
predictions and sold to anyone
who wants to influence human behaviour.

The product is not the app.
The product is not the advertisement.
The product is the prediction. #verified

---

## How It Started

Surveillance capitalism was
pioneered by Google and later
Facebook — just as mass production
capitalism was pioneered by
Ford and General Motors a century
earlier. It has become the dominant
form of information capitalism. #verified

The origin: at the turn of the
millennium Google discovered that
the seemingly inconsequential
digital traces users left —
data exhaust — could be
algorithmically processed to
predict future behaviour and
monetised through targeted advertising.

This was not planned. It was discovered.
And once discovered it was industrialised.

Former Google executive Sheryl Sandberg
moved to Facebook in 2008 and
transformed the social network's
users into revenue using the same
model. Zuboff describes her as
the figure who spread the logic
of surveillance capitalism from
Google to the broader tech industry. #verified

## The Core Mechanics

**Behavioural surplus:** #verified

Every action online generates data.
The useful data for improving a
service is the baseline.
The surplus — the data beyond what
is needed to provide the service —
is where the real commercial value lives.

Examples:
You search for a flight →
useful data: show you flights.
You search at 2am after an argument →
surplus data: emotional state
relationship stress vulnerability window.

The surplus predicts not just what
you will buy but what you will do
think feel and vote.

**Behavioural futures markets:** #verified

Zuboff's term for the new marketplaces
where predictions about human behaviour
are bought and sold. Advertisers
are not the only buyers. Political
campaigns insurance companies
financial institutions law enforcement
agencies and employers all purchase
access to behavioural prediction.

The system extends beyond advertising
into insurance employment finance
law enforcement healthcare and
political governance. #verified

**Instrumentarian power:** #credible

Zuboff's term for the form of
power surveillance capitalism exercises —
the capacity to shape direct and
control populations outside of
traditional mechanisms through
continuous algorithmically-mediated
feedback loops.

Not through force.
Not through law.
Through the invisible continuous
adjustment of what information
reaches you when and in what form.

## The Map of Your Mind

Individual data points seem harmless.
In aggregate they become something
else entirely. #verified

Your search history maps your fears.
Your music maps your emotional states.
Your location maps your relationships.
Your scroll behaviour maps your
attention vulnerabilities.
Your purchase history maps your values.
Your social graph maps your
influence network.
Your streaming choices map your
psychological state.
Your sleep patterns map your
cognitive capacity.

Combined: a predictive model of
your mind more accurate than
your own self-assessment.

This is not hypothetical.
Cambridge Analytica demonstrated
in 2016 that Facebook likes alone
could predict personality more
accurately than a person's
spouse could. #verified

The Cambridge Analytica scandal
made the invisible visible —
showing that data was not merely
collected but weaponised for
psychological manipulation.
The company's use of Facebook
data to build voter profiles
demonstrated how behavioural
prediction could be turned
into political power. #verified

## The Honest Counterpoint

The received narrative of surveillance
capitalism's technical infallibility
is often perpetuated by both
critics and advocates.

Empirical studies challenge the
claims of omnipotent surveillance:
targeting efficacy is marginal over
classical approaches and algorithmic
exclusions can reinforce data deserts
or misclassifications.

The system is not all-knowing.
It is probabilistic — making
predictions that are more accurate
than chance but far from perfect.

The concern is not that the system
is omniscient. The concern is that:
1. Even imperfect prediction at
   scale produces significant
   influence over aggregate behaviour
2. The asymmetry of knowledge —
   the corporation knows vastly more
   about you than you know about it —
   removes the conditions required
   for meaningful consent
3. The system improves continuously
   through every interaction

The target does not need to be
perfectly predicted to be effectively
influenced. #credible

## Beyond The Screen

Surveillance capitalism is no longer
confined to digital platforms.

It has extended into: #verified
- Insurance — behaviour-based pricing
  using location health and
  driving data
- Employment — algorithmic hiring
  and performance monitoring
- Finance — credit scoring using
  social and behavioural data
- Law enforcement — predictive
  policing using Palantir and
  similar systems
- Healthcare — patient behaviour
  monitoring and predictive diagnosis
- Political governance — behavioural
  microtargeting of voters

The logic that began in Google's
advertising department in 2000 now
operates in every major sector
of the economy. #verified

## Big Other

Zuboff's term for the distributed
and largely uncontested new expression
of power produced by surveillance
capitalism — a hidden architecture
of extraction commodification and
control that threatens core values
of freedom democracy and
human self-determination.

Not a centralised Big Brother
state watching from above.
A distributed Big Other embedded
in every device every platform
every connected surface — watching
from everywhere simultaneously
without a central controller. #credible

The distinction matters:
Big Brother requires a government.
Big Other requires only aligned
incentives among corporations
whose individual profit interests
produce collective surveillance
without anyone needing to design it.
