## What It Is

Stratospheric Aerosol Injection is
the deliberate introduction of
reflective particles into the
stratosphere — at altitudes of
15–25 kilometres — to reflect
a fraction of incoming solar
radiation back into space and
cool the Earth's surface. #verified

The conceptual foundation comes from
observing large volcanic eruptions.
When Mount Pinatubo erupted in 1991
it injected approximately 20 million
tonnes of sulfur dioxide into the
stratosphere. The Earth cooled by
approximately 0.5°C for over a year.

SAI would attempt to replicate this
effect artificially and continuously —
not for one year but for decades
or potentially centuries. #verified

The primary proposed substance:
sulfur dioxide — the same compound
produced by burning coal and oil
that causes acid rain at ground level.
Alumina calcite and salt are also
under consideration.

The proposed delivery mechanisms:
modified existing large jetliners
at lower altitudes or purpose-built
high-altitude aircraft for
stratospheric injection.

A 2025 Earth's Future paper
confirmed that SAI is feasible
with existing large jetliners at
lower altitudes — achieving
approximately 35% of the cooling
efficiency of high-altitude strategies.
The technical barrier to deployment
is lower than previously assumed. #verified

## SCoPEx — Cancelled Under Pressure

Harvard's SCoPEx experiment was
designed to release small amounts
of calcium carbonate particles from
a high-altitude balloon over Sweden
to test atmospheric behaviour.

Following years of sustained pressure
and campaigning by civil society
groups and Indigenous Peoples'
organisations — who were not
consulted about an experiment
that would affect their sky —
SCoPEx was cancelled in early 2024
before it successfully conducted
an outdoor experiment.

This is documented as the most
high-profile example of civil society
opposition successfully stopping
a solar geoengineering experiment.
The cancellation does not mean
SAI research has slowed.
It means one experiment was stopped
while dozens of modelling and
atmospheric studies continue globally.
