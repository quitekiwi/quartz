## The Court Order — 400 Million Users

**Nelson Mullins — July 10, 2025**
"From Copyright Case to AI Data Crisis"
https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/corporate-governance-insights/all/from-copyright-case-to-ai-data-crisis-how-the-new-york-times-v-openai-reshapes-companies-data-governance-and-ediscovery-strategy
Key findings:
- May 13, 2025: Judge Wang issued
  preservation order requiring OpenAI
  to retain ALL ChatGPT conversation
  logs — affecting 400+ million users
- First court-mandated mass preservation
  of AI content at this scale
- ChatGPT Free, Plus, Pro, and Team
  subscriptions all affected
- API users without Zero Data Retention
  agreements affected
- Conversations stored in "secured system"
  accessed only by "small, audited
  OpenAI legal security team"
- Sam Altman proposed "AI Privilege"
  to protect sensitive conversations —
  no such privilege recognised in US law

**OpenAI Official Response — June 5, 2025**
https://openai.com/new-york-times/
Confirms: preservation order scope.
New York Times demanded 1.4 billion
private ChatGPT conversations
be turned over — May 2025.
OpenAI pushing back continuously.
Court required indefinite retention
of chats users chose to delete.

## The August 2025 Privacy Incident

**Wikipedia — OpenAI**
August 2025: Thousands of private
ChatGPT conversations inadvertently
exposed to public search engines.
Experimental "share with search
engines" feature malfunctioned.
Personal details, names, locations,
intimate topics appeared in search results.
Feature permanently removed August 1, 2025.
Sam Altman acknowledged the issue.
Not a security breach — design flaw.
